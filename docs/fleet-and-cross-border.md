# Fleet, maintenance and European coordination contracts

Revision: 0.2.0 engineering candidate. This adds executable workflows while retaining the published 0.1.0-rc.1 tag and archive. Record counts, readiness claims and agreements originate from accountable user inputs; no aircraft or maintenance history is seeded into an operational installation.

## Maintenance limits and work

`fleet.MaintenanceProgram` contains versioned tasks with source references and optional calendar-day, flight-second and flight-cycle intervals. At least one actual reviewed interval is required. No universal UAS maintenance limit is supplied. A matching independently reviewed completion establishes a task baseline; measured utilisation must identify the same aircraft/configuration, cannot precede its baseline counters, and cannot reset itself through a planning proposal. Missing baselines, unreviewed programs, open safety defects, expired instructions and mismatched configuration block readiness. The earliest applicable trigger governs. Reaching a limit during the proposed flight remains blocked.

The scheduled-utilisation forecast combines the proposed assignment with other still-reserved flights before its completion, using exact reserved flight durations and one planned flight cycle per assigned flight. This is a conservative allocation calculation, not a flown-flight record. Duplicate local/physical-ledger representations are counted once. Actual measured counter updates and their evidence remain separate.

Saved utilisation and imported released-completion baselines identify `evidence_ref` and its exact `evidence_version`. Draft calculators can represent a missing revision, but reservation and dispatch require the actual current tenant-owned evidence to be independently reviewed, Approved, and bound to that revision, with provenance and SHA-256 metadata. Suspended, revised or missing sources cannot be reused for a new release. Equally recent completion records with conflicting counters block assessment. An owner source change also suspends an Approved dependent maintenance program, with a new version and audit record; neither later source approval nor agreement acceptance silently restores that program.

`plan_maintenance` uses CP-SAT optional intervals, qualified technician availability, facility task scope/capacity and aircraft exclusions. A separately implemented verifier recomputes the proposal. Solver statuses distinguish feasible, optimal, infeasible and unknown results. These are proposals; saving a plan does not record work as performed.

Work orders follow `Draft → Scheduled → In Progress → Work Complete → Under Review → Released`. Only unstarted work may be cancelled. The dedicated transition endpoint enforces current aircraft/program/resource versions, source task scope, qualified technicians, facility capacity and existing aircraft reservations. The assigned actual technician records start and completion; an actual end remains missing while work is in progress. Completion evidence must include its measured counters and content provenance. A qualified assigned reviewer independent of the preparer/performer records release, including a second reviewer when configured. A task release does not charge a battery, clear unrelated defects, approve a flight or silently restore the aircraft's separate readiness record. Started maintenance marks the aircraft suspended and invalidates its previous readiness version; a new readiness review is required.

Starting work rechecks both the assigned technician and facility's current approved revisions. Controlled work completion binds the actual evidence revision; independent release binds the current Approved evidence. Maintenance signatures identify the exact work, configuration/program/aircraft versions and evidence revisions. Returning or replacing completion evidence clears earlier signatures. Changed evidence or a changed earlier reviewer's qualification prevents those signatures from being counted toward a later release.

Saved data contracts:

| Record | Controlled data |
| --- | --- |
| Aircraft | `scheduler`, `maintenance_program_id`, `utilisation`, `maintenance_completions`, `open_safety_defects` |
| Maintenance program | `program` with server-owned ID/version/status and review actor |
| Maintenance work order | `work_order`, assigned `reviewer_ids`; state changes use dedicated control |
| Maintenance personnel | `user_id`, `maintenance_technician` availability, qualification expiries and evidence |
| Maintenance facility | Site record with `maintenance_facility` capacity, task scope, availability and evidence |

GET `/api/v1/fleet/aircraft/{id}/maintenance` aggregates existing tenant records. POST to its `/assess` suffix accepts only assessment time and explicit forecast use; it cannot replace saved program/history with invented inputs. POST `/api/v1/fleet/work-orders/{id}/control` requires record version, target and rationale, plus actual scheduling/completion records where relevant. Aircraft maintenance in another organisation remains controlled by the owner; a borrowed alias cannot change the owner's serviceability.

## Country segments and local conditions

The geographic catalogue contains 50 jurisdiction codes, each initially **Registered**, with no default permission or operational-validation assertion. EU Member States, EASA states outside the EU and other European frameworks remain distinct. EASA membership alone does not switch on EU applicability. Every operation requires reviewed country-pack/application evidence with valid dates; no software-built country entry asserts that its national law or geographical zones are complete.

Each mission's `coordination` plan contains a registration country, operator organisation, aircraft/route baseline, full operation interval and contiguous country segments. Segment records reference the actual reviewed country-pack version and scoped instruments, local conditions, mitigations and source freshness. Every actual border transition needs a responsible handover, procedure, acknowledgement and contingency-coordination evidence. Missing intervals, overlapping segments, expired or wrong-operator/configuration/route/country instruments and incomplete handovers block readiness.

Operational-authorisation, STS-declaration and LUC routes retain different cross-border evidence. Under the preserved Article 13(2) text, the operational-authorisation route requires a host-authority `host_confirmation` concerning the updated local mitigations. Article 13(3) instead requires the operator to provide the host authority copies of its declaration and the home authority's confirmation of receipt and completeness. That route records `declaration_home_receipt` plus `host_declaration_submission`, with actual documented provision evidence, home-origin `issuing_country`, host `recipient_country` and `provided_at`. It does not invent a second host-issued receipt or approval. Article 13(4) similarly records the underlying `luc_terms` and operator `luc_host_notification` providing the terms of approval and intended locations. Its documented delivery evidence and recipient/time remain explicit, without demanding an invented host response. Internal approval of such evidence reviews a record; it does not issue an authority instrument.

These records remain separate from the underlying legal basis and U-space response. Open-category applicability does not invent an operational-authorisation requirement. Non-EU frameworks require reviewed national/adoption evidence rather than receiving blanket EU permissions. This implements evidence checking; it does not calculate national airspace boundaries or submit documents.

## Explicit bilateral sharing

Only an owner fleet manager/reviewer can prepare and offer its own reviewed aircraft baseline. The named counterpart explicitly accepts or rejects the terms. A private draft is invisible to the counterpart. Terms identify both organisations, actual owner aircraft/version/configuration, agreement reference, dates, countries, routes, purposes, maintenance responsibility, insurance and data-sharing evidence. Acceptance transfers no operating authorisation, pilot privilege, provider activation or aircraft command authority.

The counterpart sees the agreed terms and a fixed allowlist of asset-readiness claims clipped to the agreement interval. Owner evidence contents, customers, correspondence, personnel records and unrelated operations are not copied. The operating organisation prepares its own reviewed aircraft alias, personnel, maintenance and legal records. The alias identifies the explicit agreement, owner organisation and owner aircraft. Both the operator's and contracted owner's current maintenance readiness are checked internally; unavailable owner evidence returns a generic blocker without disclosing its content.

An existing alias cannot remove or change its agreement/owner/asset binding through ordinary editing, and an owned record cannot be converted into a borrowed alias that way. New aliases require acceptance by the named counterpart and matching current owner/configuration records. Material ownership transfer needs a separate controlled process; the platform does not attest external legal ownership or manufacture a universal aircraft registry. Owner maintenance-program, instruction, utilisation-source and completion-source revisions invalidate internal releases only for explicitly linked assets and physical-ledger counterparties. Private source records are retained in the owner's tenant.

A canonical physical-resource ledger prevents owner and borrowers from booking the same aircraft concurrently or allocating the same readiness flight-time budget repeatedly. Organisation locks have a fixed order, followed by canonical resource locks; identities and scope are rechecked after locks. Owner configuration/readiness changes invalidate version-bound offers. Existing 0.1 reservations are retained when checking availability and budgets. Revocation blocks internal release and suspends physical exclusions; it does not silently reallocate an aircraft or send a provider withdrawal. Explicit operator schedule cancellation releases its reservations with an audit trail.

These concurrency mechanisms have software tests using SQLite. `tests/test_postgres_coordination.py` adds real PostgreSQL API/transaction and concurrent owner/borrower tests in disposable `verification_<uuid>` schemas whose search path excludes `public`. Without the explicit isolated database URL those tests skip. PostgreSQL execution and deployment-specific transaction/failure testing remain separate acceptance requirements until actual results are recorded.

## Internal dispatch

The actual assigned pilot account acknowledges an exact versioned reserved assignment. Acknowledgement cannot be granted by another pilot or a preparer acting as reviewer. A separate assigned independent reviewer then requests internal dispatch release. It rechecks current reserved snapshots, legal instruments/issuer evidence, country segments, maintenance, owner agreement, qualifications, safety-case review, approved dispatch policy and the exact pilot acknowledgement. The approved operator policy supplies review lead time and validity; no arbitrary dispatch lead window is embedded.

Dispatch requires all three local resource reservations and the canonical physical-aircraft reservation for the exact current assignment. A partially missing, suspended or inconsistent ledger requires reconciliation. A documented Article 13 submission dated after the current review time cannot support a current dispatch release, even if that date would precede the future operation.

Configured second review applies to this checkpoint. Returned state is **Internally Released**, with a validity timestamp and retained input/version bindings. Provider activation remains **Not Performed**, flight execution **Not Recorded**, and aircraft command capability **false**. Any actual execution record or provider action belongs to a separate authorised integration. Saved release status must be interpreted with its expiry and current input versions; it is not a perpetual permission.

No external correspondence, authority contact or emergency-service action is performed by these workflows.
